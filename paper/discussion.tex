\paragraph{What the transformer computes.} In all three settings the trained models are good in-context system
identifiers. They match the exact Bayes predictor at short contexts, beat physics-feature regression on contact
dynamics, and adapt within a few steps. Their \emph{form} of identification is not the one we pre-registered. There is no
linearly readable, causally used parameter vector in the residual stream. The estimate sits in the key--value
memory of the last two blocks, and attention applies it to the query, so it shows up as the input--output Jacobian of the model.
Remark~\ref{rem:fw} explains why this is a natural solution for near-uniform heads, and Proposition~\ref{prop:construct}
explains why two attention layers suffice to gather the required statistics. For interpretability of adaptive
controllers (and of RMA-style policies), the lesson is to probe the \emph{function} (Jacobians) and the
\emph{memory} (keys and values), not only the activations.

\paragraph{Why pre-registration mattered.} Without it, a residual probe with $R^2{=}\ARtheta$ and a significant (but
negligible) gain over the truth (H2b) could easily have been written up as ``the model represents its estimate''.
The pre-registered thresholds and the causal tests H3a/H3b rejected that story. The alternative was then found with
exploratory tools. We label it as exploratory and test it causally (KV patching, Fig.~\ref{fig:mech}c). We also test
it out-of-sample, on models trained after the hypothesis was formed: the bouncing-ball system and the scale study.

\paragraph{Limitations.} (i)~\emph{Scale.} The models have 0.2--3M parameters, the linear systems have at most 24
unknowns, and the MuJoCo systems have one degree of freedom. Whether the key--value account holds for large
robot foundation models is open. (ii)~\emph{Post-hoc mechanism.} The fast-weight account was formulated after the
pre-registered probes failed. Its main evidence comes from the same models, with out-of-sample support from new models
only. (iii)~\emph{First-order picture.} Remark~\ref{rem:fw} linearises softmax attention. The heads are near-uniform
but not exactly, and the account explains roughly $90\%$ of the transferable behaviour, not all of it. (iv)~\emph{Approximation
floor.} The predictors stay a factor \Aratio\ above the Bayes risk, averaged over contexts (H1c rejected). \PENDING{scaleclaim} (v)~\emph{Control recipe.} Pure closed-loop BPTT, as
pre-registered, did not train reliably on open-loop-unstable plants. Our controller conclusions are about a DAgger
warm start followed by cost fine-tuning, and other recipes (e.g.\ RL) may behave differently. (vi)~\emph{Authorship.}
This is single-author independent work produced with substantial AI assistance (see below). Every number is regenerated by
the public scripts, and the pre-registration and deviation log are public.
