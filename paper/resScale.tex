\section{Scale, seeds and a pre-registered confirmation}\label{sec:scale}
Table~\ref{tab:scale} (appendix; one seed per row, exploratory) asks whether the approximation floor of
\S\ref{sec:expa} is an artefact of a small model or short training, and whether the Jacobian account survives a
larger system. \emph{The floor is partly an optimisation and capacity limit.} Tripling the training steps lowers the
mean excess ratio to Bayes from \Aratio\ to \ScLongRatio\ (at $t{=}47$ from \AratioEnd\ to \ScLongRatioEnd), doubling
the depth gives \ScDeepRatio\ and doubling the width (4$\times$ the parameters) \ScWideRatio, while a 0.1M-parameter model is far off (\ScSmallRatio);
none reaches the Bayes risk. \emph{The functional account holds across sizes:} at $t{=}47$ the query Jacobian matches the
ridge estimate with $R^2\ge\ScMinBigJacEnd$ for the long-trained, deep and wide models (small model: \ScSmallJacEnd). \emph{On a larger system} ($n{=}4$, $m{=}2$, 24 unknowns) the transformer is far from
Bayes (excess ratio \ScFourRatio) yet its Jacobian still matches the ridge estimate ($R^2{=}\ScFourJacEnd$ at $t{=}47$);
the Bayes risk follows the finite-sample form of Corollary~\ref{cor:law}, $t\,\mathcal{R}^\star_{47}{=}\ScFourTR$ against
$np\sigma^2\,t/(t{-}p{-}1){=}\ScFourFinite$. \emph{Seeds.} Seven further training seeds of the main model, trained after the key--value account was formed,
replicate the behaviour (mean excess ratio \RepRatio, mean $\pm$ s.d.\ over \RepN\ seeds), the localisation
(KV-only patching of blocks 3--4 transfers \RepKv; minimum over seeds \RepKvMin), and the readability gap: the fast
weights read $\hat\theta_{47}$ with \RepFwTheta\ (minimum \RepFwMin), the residual stream with \RepResQ\ at the query and
\RepResCtx\ over all positions; the residual readout varies more across seeds and reaches \RepResMax\ in the best one.
\emph{A pre-registered confirmation.} Before looking inside the long-trained, deep and wide models we registered
(tag \texttt{prereg2-94b6cf8}, Appendix~\ref{app:confirm}) that replacing only the context keys and values of the second half of
the blocks transfers $\tau{\ge}0.8$, block~1 alone $\tau{\le}0.3$, and that at matched dimension the fast weights read
$\hat\theta_{47}$ at least 0.15 better than the residual stream. All \CfNsix\ tests hold (Holm over 11): $\tau{=}\CfTauLate$,
block~1 \CfTauOne, $R^2$ \CfKv\ against \CfRes. The 0.1M-parameter model is the boundary: its fast weights read worse than
its residual stream (gap \CfSmallGap). A prior shift tests the algorithm: with the $A$ entries four times smaller, the query
Jacobian stays closer to ridge with the \emph{training} prior than to the Bayes estimate for the new prior (mean squared
distance \CfDTrain\ vs.\ \CfDNew, $t{\ge}8$) or to OLS (\CfDOls), in \CfSevenaN\ of 10 seeds. Within one episode the network
cannot know the population changed, so a fixed Bayes estimator must do this; the margin to the new-prior estimate is
small because the two estimates differ little once $t{\ge}8$.
