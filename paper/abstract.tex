Sequence models that adapt to unknown dynamics from their own history are common in robot learning, but what they
compute is unclear. We study small transformers trained from scratch, in a pre-registered design with exact optimal
references and causal interventions, in the smallest settings where the question has exact answers: linear systems
with unknown parameters, one-degree-of-freedom MuJoCo systems with contact, and linear adaptive control. The predictors
are Bayes-optimal while data are scarce, then settle at an approximation floor (\Aratio$\times$ the Bayes excess risk).
Our pre-registered mechanism, a linearly readable and causally used parameter estimate in the residual stream, is
rejected (probe $R^2{=}\ARtheta$; steering and patching have no effect). Exploratory analyses locate the sufficient
statistics of the context in the attention key--value memory of the last two blocks: they are read from it with
$R^2{=}\AcFwMom$, against \AcResBestMom\ for the best residual-stream readout, and replacing only those keys and values
with another episode's transfers $\AkvThreeFour$ of its behaviour. Physical parameters in MuJoCo are also more readable from the key--value memory than from the residual stream, by margins that vary with the system. A
controller trained by imitation and then on closed-loop cost excites the plant without an identification objective,
though less than dithered certainty equivalence, and is competitive in median cost but fails on \CTFfail\% of plants,
almost all open-loop unstable, against \CCEDfail\%. Code, pre-registration and every number are public.
