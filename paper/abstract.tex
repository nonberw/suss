Sequence models that adapt to unknown dynamics from their own history are widely used in robot learning, but
what they compute is unclear. We study this question in small transformers trained from scratch, in a
pre-registered design with exact optimal references and causal interventions, in the smallest settings where the
question has exact answers: linear systems with unknown parameters, one-degree-of-freedom MuJoCo systems with
contact, and linear adaptive control. The trained predictors are Bayes-optimal while data are scarce, then settle at
an approximation floor. Our pre-registered mechanism, a linearly readable and causally used parameter estimate in the
residual stream, is rejected: the best linear probe reaches $R^2{=}\ARtheta$, and steering and patching along it have no
effect. Exploratory analyses, labelled as such, locate the context information in the attention key--value memory of
the last two blocks: replacing only those keys and values with another episode's transfers $\AkvThreeFour$ of its
behaviour, while the same operation on the first two blocks transfers $\AkvOne$; the predictor is near-linear in its
query, and its input Jacobian matches the Bayes estimate ($R^2{=}\AjacBayes$). In MuJoCo, physical parameters are
likewise more readable from the key--value memory than from the residual stream, most clearly on the pendulum. A
transformer controller trained by imitation and then on closed-loop cost learns to excite the plant without any
identification objective, though less than dithered certainty equivalence (\CinfoCED\ nats less); its median cost is
comparable to classical adaptive controllers, but it fails on \CTFfail\% of plants, almost all open-loop unstable,
against \CCEDfail\% for dithered certainty equivalence. Code, pre-registration, deviation log and every number are public.
